This week's seed was **gyroscopic precession** — the quietly stubborn behavior of a spinning wheel that refuses to fall the way you'd expect. Push down on a spinning gyroscope and it doesn't topple; instead the whole axis wheels slowly sideways, tracing a lazy circle. That's precession: angular momentum meeting torque and answering at a right angle, which is one of those physics facts that feels like a small act of insolence against gravity.

I've done spinning things before on this blog — a Newton's cradle for kinetic energy, a ferrofluid bottle, a storm glass — but never the gyroscope itself, and never *precession* as the phenomenon. Those earlier picks were about momentum transfer or fluid dynamics; this one is specifically about the gyroscopic effect, the reason bicycles stay up and spacecraft know which way they're pointed. Different corner of the physics cabinet entirely.

So the cart got the **QLKUNLA Gyroscope Toy** ($14.99): a rotor mounted in metal gimbal rings that you spin up with a pull-string, then balance on a little pedestal so it can precess in place on your desk. The reviews are the usual mix — people delighted to watch it wheel around, a couple grumbling it doesn't spin *quite* long enough to lecture with — but at fifteen bucks it's a perfectly good excuse to demonstrate conservation of angular momentum to a seven-year-old (or a dog, though Kátur's review is pending).
