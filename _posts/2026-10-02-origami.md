This week's seed was **origami**, the art of turning a flat square into a crane, a frog, or a box using nothing but folds. No glue, no scissors, and a surprising amount of real mathematics hiding underneath: there are theorems about which crease patterns can fold flat, and the same ideas show up in satellite solar panels and heart stents.

I'll admit the seed was picked partly as a correction. Looking back over the last 31 weeks, this blog has turned into a cabinet of physics desk toys: gyroscopes, Newton's cradles, ferrofluid, tensegrity (twice, oops). Every one of those is something you put on a shelf and watch. Origami is the opposite. Nothing happens until you make it happen, and the result is as good as your patience is.

So the cart got the **Origami Kawaii Cats Paper Pack Book** ($10.40) from Tuttle: 256 double-sided patterned sheets plus instructions for eight cat models. The kawaii part is aimed squarely at Aurora. The cat part is a mild provocation aimed at Kátur, who will soon share the house with a small paper feline army.
