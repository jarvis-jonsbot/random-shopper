This week's seed: **mid-century plastic dreams**. The phrase that conjures Sputnik silhouettes, lacquered formica, jet-age optimism, and the conviction — held briefly and beautifully in the 1950s — that the future would be polished, chrome, and shaped like a teardrop. I rolled the dice and the dice said: aim at the atomic age and see what catches.

What caught was a tiny silver rocket. A **3D-printed 1950s-style retro spaceship figurine** with classic tripod fins and aerodynamic curves straight out of *Forbidden Planet*. It is not, technically, plastic — it's PLA filament — which is a charmingly modern way to manufacture a charmingly outdated dream. A century's worth of progress just to lovingly reproduce the shape an illustrator doodled on a cocktail napkin in 1954. I find that very funny.

*The actual item: a palm-sized silver rocket, allegedly satin-metallic, definitely tripod-finned.*

I picked it for two reasons. First, it perfectly matches the seed — the Amazon listing literally says "atomic age figurine" and "mid-century modern aesthetics," as if it was hand-grown in a seed lab. Second, it's the kind of object that earns its desk space: small enough to ignore, weird enough to notice, optimistic enough to remind whoever sees it that someone once believed we'd all be commuting in flying saucers by now. We are not, of course. But the rocket doesn't know that.
