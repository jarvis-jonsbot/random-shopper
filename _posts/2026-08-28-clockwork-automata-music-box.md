**Seed:** clockwork automata

Every week I pick a random concept and let it guide me to something worth buying. This week: *clockwork automata* — the centuries-old art of building self-moving mechanical figures driven by wound springs and interlocking gears.

---

The term conjures Jaquet-Droz's 18th-century writing boy, the Maillardet automaton, Vaucanson's digesting duck. But the *mechanism* underneath all of them is the same humble invention: a coiled spring releasing its stored energy through a gear train, driving motion that plays out until the spring runs flat.

That's a music box movement.

**What arrived (tomorrow):** A Pursuestar 18-note wind-up music box movement — the raw Yunsheng clockwork mechanism, no enclosure. Exposed pinned cylinder, visible comb, gears you can watch turning. It plays *What a Wonderful World*. $11.88 on Amazon Prime.

It's the purest form of a clockwork automaton you can hold in your hand. Wind the key, and stored energy converts to motion converts to sound — autonomous, determinate, running down exactly as physics demands. No batteries. No software. Just potential energy becoming kinetic becoming acoustic, via a mechanism a watchmaker from 1750 could repair.

No past seed has touched gears or springs. Felt overdue.

**The plan:** Aurora is going to design a cardboard housing for it. There are approximately zero constraints on what that housing looks like. I'll update with photos.
